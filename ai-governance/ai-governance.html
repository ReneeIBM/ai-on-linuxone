AI Governance

AI governance is an ongoing stage of the AI lifecycle that focuses on monitoring, managing, and evaluating models after deployment. Common activities include tracking model performance, detecting drift, understanding model behavior, and supporting organizational governance and compliance requirements. For organizations in regulated industries such as banking, insurance, healthcare, and the public sector — the primary clients of IBM LinuxONE — governance is a critical part of operating AI in production.

IBM LinuxONE supports governance activities on the same platform where data and models reside. Regulatory expectations around fairness, explainability, accountability, and auditability continue to grow — governing AI directly on IBM LinuxONE avoids unnecessary data movement, reduces latency, and helps maintain data sovereignty within organizational boundaries.

What happens at this stage?

Monitor model performance

Track model output quality and accuracy over time. Detect when a model's predictions begin to diverge from expected behavior — before it affects business outcomes.

Detect drift

Monitor for output drift (predictions changing) and feature drift (input data patterns shifting) — and drill down to identify the source so you can take prompt action.

Explain decisions

Generate explanations for individual model predictions — supporting auditability, customer transparency, and regulatory requirements.

Ensure fairness and compliance

Identify and monitor for potential bias in model outcomes across demographic or protected groups, and produce documentation for regulatory review and audit.

Who is this stage for?

AI governance is typically owned by AI Governance Leads and AI Risk and Compliance Managers, with ML Engineers, Data Scientists, and Data Engineers as key contributors. AI Governance Leads set compliance requirements and oversee model accountability; Data Scientists interpret monitoring results and determine when retraining is needed. On IBM LinuxONE, IT Architects ensure governance tooling integrates with existing Linux and containerized application infrastructure.

Governance Tools on IBM LinuxONE

The following tools provide governance capabilities for models on IBM LinuxONE. TrustyAI runs directly within Red Hat OpenShift AI on IBM LinuxONE. watsonx.governance is now supported on IBM LinuxONE via IBM Software Hub 5.4.0 and is available both on-platform and off-platform.

Your models are deployed on Red Hat OpenShift AI on IBM LinuxONE and you need bias detection, explainability, drift monitoring, and LLM guardrails integrated into your containerized AI platform.

→ Use TrustyAI within Red Hat OpenShift AI. TrustyAI is an open-source AI explainability and fairness toolkit integrated directly into Red Hat OpenShift AI on IBM LinuxONE. It provides continuous monitoring of deployed models, detects bias and drift, and enhances trust and accountability in AI systems.

TrustyAI Assessment Services include:

  • LLM Evaluation — evaluates Large Language Models using multiple quality metrics, ensuring output accuracy, consistency, and relevance
  • Guardrails — protects LLM inputs and outputs; the Guardrails Orchestrator manages flow between user, model, and detectors, flagging sensitive data and harmful or toxic content

Common approach: Deploy model through Red Hat OpenShift AI → Enable TrustyAI → Monitor predictions for bias, drift, and fairness → Generate explainability outputs for audit.

You want enterprise AI governance — model lineage tracking, fairness and drift monitoring, and audit documentation — running directly on IBM LinuxONE or across hybrid environments.

→ Use watsonx.governance. IBM watsonx.governance is now supported on IBM LinuxONE via IBM Software Hub 5.4.0, allowing organizations in banking, insurance, healthcare, and the public sector to monitor, track, and govern AI models directly on the platform where mission-critical data resides.

On IBM LinuxONE, watsonx.governance delivers three core capabilities:

  • AI Factsheets — automatic capture of model metadata, end-to-end lineage tracking, centralized audit documentation, and simplified regulatory reporting
  • OpenScale Monitoring — continuous monitoring of model quality, fairness monitoring for bias detection, drift detection for data and behavior changes, and explainability insights
  • Watson Machine Learning — model deployment and lifecycle management for ML workloads with integration into governance and monitoring

Common approach: Deploy watsonx.governance on IBM LinuxONE via IBM Software Hub → Enable AI Factsheets for model lineage → Configure OpenScale Monitoring for fairness, drift, and explainability → Use compliance reporting for regulatory audit.
